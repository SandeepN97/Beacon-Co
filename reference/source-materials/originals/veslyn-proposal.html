The two most contestable picks, checked against real evidence

job queue

pg-boss stays — it's the more popular pick, not just the incumbent. Checked it against Graphile Worker, the other serious Postgres-native queue: pg-boss carries more GitHub stars (3,695 vs ~2,300) and nearly 3× the weekly npm downloads (754k vs 260k). It also now ships an official dashboard package. No change needed — the original choice was already the better-adopted one.

uptime monitoring

Swapped BetterStack for Uptime Kuma. 85,000+ GitHub stars, MIT-licensed — more starred than Grafana, Prometheus, or Netdata. Unlimited monitors and public status pages for the cost of a small VPS instead of a monthly SaaS bill. Honest trade-off: it checks from one location (wherever it's hosted), where a paid service checks from many — acceptable here since Cloudflare's own edge is the thing actually serving traffic.

error tracking

Sentry stays. GlitchTip is a real, Sentry-SDK-compatible open-source option worth knowing — but self-hosting only pays for itself north of roughly a million events a month. This business won't be near that volume for a long time, if ever. Sentry's free tier is the correct call today.

07 · The Automation Layer — Website & Social, Tool by Tool

How content gets made with almost no one touching it.

Two pipelines run this business: one turns a calendar slot into a posted video or graphic, the other turns a static build into a site that feels alive. Both are designed around the same rule — automate everything except the one decision that actually needs a human. Checked against how real agency automations are actually built — n8n's own template library runs the exact same idea-to-post shape — and against real production motion-design writeups, which converge on the same restraint principle this proposal already argued: one memorable moment beats decorating everything.

What actually earns a place here — checked against real GitHub adoption

website motion

Keep Motion, add GSAP for one job. GSAP went fully free — including ScrollTrigger and DrawSVG — after Webflow's 2025 acquisition, and its GitHub repo sits at 24k+ stars with roughly 12 million live sites. DrawSVG specifically does the self-drawing-sketch effect natively, more robustly than the hand-rolled stroke-dashoffset trick this proposal's own sketches use. Verdict: Motion stays for the pervasive light touches (it's 2.3kb — GSAP would be overkill everywhere), GSAP gets reserved for the one signature scroll/draw moment, same "one deliberate flourish" rule as the 3D hero.

video render

JSON2Video for Phase 1, Remotion as the credible upgrade. Remotion carries 54k GitHub stars, Apache-2.0 licensed, renders real MP4 from React components — meaning it could share the exact same design tokens as the Astro site. It didn't replace JSON2Video today because self-hosting it means running your own headless-Chrome render worker, a real infra + engineering cost JSON2Video's managed API was specifically bought to avoid. Worth revisiting once volume justifies the switch.

character / avatar generation

Researched, deliberately not adopted. Even the best-starred open options (LivePortrait, MuseTalk, Wav2Lip) top out around 1.5k stars and universally need a GPU — a different infrastructure class than everything else in this stack, which runs serverless on Cloudflare and Supabase. And for a barbershop or bakery, an AI avatar reading a script works against the "honest, local, human" positioning this whole proposal is built on. Real photos beat synthetic faces here.

thumbnails

09 · Designed the Way Eyes Actually Move

Not neuromarketing — eye-tracking research, applied honestly.

These are two different disciplines, and it's worth being precise. True neuromarketing measures physiological response directly — EEG, galvanic skin response, fMRI — expensive, lab-based, not something a small agency deploys. Eye-tracking is different: decades of published, peer-reviewed research on where people actually look, freely available and directly applicable to how a page gets built. This section is that second thing, done honestly.

This proposal applies published eye-tracking research to layout decisions — it does not claim to run its own lab studies. Every finding below is sourced; the rules that follow are how Veslyn turns that research into what actually ships.
NIELSEN NORMAN GROUP · ORIGINAL 2006 STUDY, 232 USERS · RECONFIRMED 2017 ON MOBILE

The F-pattern is real — and it's a warning sign, not a design goal

NN/g's eye-tracking found people scan web content in a rough F-shape: a horizontal sweep across the top, a shorter sweep further down, then a vertical scan down the left edge. But NN/g's own follow-up research is explicit about what this means: the F-pattern is negative for users and businesses, and good design can prevent it. It shows up on pages that are dense walls of text — not a pattern to design around, a pattern good hierarchy makes disappear. On an average page, users read only 20–28% of the words present.

VWO / TECHWYSE CASE STUDY

The badge that stole the button's job

A heatmap on a real landing page showed a non-clickable "No-Fee" badge pulling the most attention on the entire screen — while the actual call-to-action button, sitting right next to it, got a fraction of the traction. Removing the badge fixed the hierarchy. Nothing about the CTA itself changed; only what was competing with it.

JUST & CARPENTER, 1980 · COGNITIVE PSYCHOLOGY

How long an eye lingers tells you something

Short fixations mean scanning — the normal, healthy way people move through a page. Long, lingering fixations tend to mean something else: confusion, or a person working harder than they should to understand what's in front of them. A page that generates lots of long fixations near your call-to-action is a page that's making people think when it should be making things obvious.

GAZE-CUEING RESEARCH · MULTIPLE STUDIES, PRESENT WITH THE HONEST CAVEAT

The "person looking at your button" trick — real, but oversold

There's a genuine, well-documented phenomenon called gaze cueing — people reflexively glance where another person's eyes point. But direct, camera-facing gaze is also independently attention-grabbing on its own. The honest version: a real photo of a real person is powerful; exactly where their eyes point is a smaller, secondary lever, not the guaranteed conversion hack marketing blogs often sell it as.

What the scarce resource actually is

Not money — time. Post-automation delivery is budgeted at 2–3 hours per client per month. Fifteen clients ≈ 35–45 hours monthly, compatible with full-time employment through Phase 2. The 18-client cap is a design constraint, not a hope.

Why incumbents can't follow the price down

Every dossiered competitor bills hours against payroll. Matching $450/month with human-delivered social content loses them money on day one. Veslyn's floor is $31; theirs is a salary. That asymmetry is the moat — the automation stack, not the price tag.

Results compound — reviews accumulate and rankings climb, so leaving means losing momentum, not just a vendor. 14 · Risks, Honestly

What could kill this, and what's already aimed at each.

Solo-operator dependency

Everything routes through one person. Mitigation: automation-first delivery, runbooks from day one, client-owns-files continuity.

Platform API shifts (Meta · TikTok · Google)

One policy change can break a pipeline. Mitigation: multi-platform spread; approval-queue architecture degrades to manual posting instead of failing.

Time collision with full-time employment

The realistic failure mode is overcommitment. Mitigation: hard 18-client cap, ≤3 hr/client target, phase gates that only add clients after hours drop.
